# River structure acts through incoming clocks and the shared downstream response

## Research result

The same real river corridor can transmit a nearly intact pulse, separate its
incoming peaks, or produce a lower and longer downstream response. Its path
arrangement interacts with the source pulse clocks; its shared-channel response
then acts on the combined signal. **River form matters through these operations,
with substantial variation within each of the original three forms.**

This version completes 33,984 controlled concentration scenarios on 295 selected
real network junctions and 59 monitored footprints. It retains the original
form labels, representative maps, two no-junction exclusions and 22 original
covariate-selected elongated/broad pairs. No prediction model was trained.

## 1. Incoming clocks can reveal or cancel path-induced separation

The two source pulses have identical heights, durations and concentration-time
integrals. Their phases compensate, leave unchanged or reinforce the real branch
path differences. The flow-weighted source phase remains zero, so changing source
clocks does not move the mean input event.

For the middle-duration input (SD=0.15), with the shared-channel reference volume
and discharge, give each of the 295 selected junctions equal weight:

| Incoming timing | Outlet/input peak with translation only | Outlet/input peak with the prescribed distributed response | Extra reduction from the distributed response (%) | Central 80% duration change from that response (%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sources compensate paths: synchronous at junction |1.000|0.752|24.82|+55.72|
| Simultaneous sources: real paths set separation |0.857|0.659|22.23|+44.64|
| Source clocks reinforce path separation |0.787|0.609|21.92|+34.14|

Natural path-induced separation lowers the mean peak by **14.32%** relative to
aligned arrivals. Reinforcing it lowers the mean peak by **21.29%**. Conversely,
compensating input clocks makes all translation-only peaks equal to one: the
geometry has not disappeared, but its differences no longer separate the arrivals.

The distributed-response fraction is fixed at 50% of shared mean time. It is a
controlled conservative kernel, not a measured 50% attenuation. For simultaneous
sources its extra reduction is **22.23%**, HUC4-block interval **20.28–24.28%**, and
its duration increase is **44.64%**, interval **38.56–51.62%**. These percentages
compare with the translation-only response under the same input clocks; they
must not be added to the 14.32% path reduction. The combined mean output/input
peak is 0.659, corresponding to 34.10% mean reduction from aligned pure translation.

The controlled statement is therefore concrete: **which tributaries meet when
can amplify or cancel the peak-separating role of the same paths**. No incoming
land-cover or source-composition contrast is needed to generate that effect.

## 2. A longer mean transit is different from a wider transit distribution

Shared mean time is scaled by relative volume divided by relative discharge.
Pure translation changes the centroid and peak clock without changing peak
height or duration. With a distributed response, changing that ratio also changes
the response width. This experiment lets mean time vary; the earlier storage
placement study instead preserved mean arrival.

For simultaneous sources, middle-duration input and 50% distributed response:

| Relative shared volume | Relative discharge | Extra peak reduction from the distributed response (%) | Central duration increase (%) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|1|1|22.23|44.64|
|1|2|10.69|14.73|
|2|1|37.60|119.17|
|2|2|22.23|44.64|

Doubling both quantities gives exactly the reference result. Thus a larger
channel volume does not imply the same increase in transit opportunity under
every discharge condition. Branch routing delays remain fixed here to isolate
the shared-channel operation; the calculation is not a full hydraulic simulation.
The concentration-time integral is preserved. The imposed carbon load is larger
at larger constant discharge, so this is identical concentration forcing rather
than an equal-carbon-mass comparison between discharge settings.

## 3. Return the response to the original river forms

The selected junction set contains **102 elongated**, **28 sparse/mainstem** and
**165 broad-branching** instances. It represents two major source paths at each
junction, with median selected area share **83.15%** in the parent geometry
analysis; it does not replace the earlier experiment with inputs over every
incremental catchment.

At simultaneous-source input and the reference distributed response, mean
outlet/input peaks are 0.637, 0.572 and 0.688 for elongated, sparse and broad forms.
All three have wide within-form distributions. These descriptive means retain
different common-path proportions and branch shares; the class name does not
standardize the shared response opportunity.

The original **22 environment-matched elongated/broad pairs** give a more focused
comparison. Under the same reference setting, elongated minus broad central
duration is **+0.126 relative time units**, HUC4-block interval **+0.044 to +0.199**.
Their peak difference is −0.037, interval −0.104 to +0.033. With translation only,
the duration difference is +0.097, interval +0.004 to +0.189, while the peak
difference is near zero. The retained pair evidence is stronger for longer
response duration than for a stable peak ranking in these selected corridors.

The real-form figure keeps complete mapped river outlines, highlights the selected
incoming branches and shared trunk, and shows both representative curves and
within-form distributions. Compensating input clocks collapses branch-arrival
dispersion, while a common downstream response can still differentiate corridors.

## 4. What the five field junctions add

The fall channel surveys allow an additional hydraulic diagnostic. We average
width*mean-observed-depth within each surveyed transect, then give transects equal
weight within reach. This preserves width/depth covariance rather than multiplying
two separately averaged reach values. The product remains a rectangular proxy,
not an exact velocity-area measurement of an irregular cross-section.

| Junction | Receiver/mainstem rectangular area proxy | Receiver/mainstem discharge | Same-length transit proxy | Published tracer transit ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|Con-1|1.822|1.091|1.670|2.551|
|Con-2|2.984|2.905|1.027|1.126|
|Con-3|1.911|1.337|1.430|0.915|
|Con-4|1.454|1.186|1.225|1.173|
|Con-5|1.429|1.089|1.313|1.051|

At Con-2, the area-proxy increase is largely offset by added discharge. At Con-3,
the proxy and reported tracer transit even fall on opposite sides of one. Survey
shape, obstructions, scale and sampling support remain consequential. The 31
missing obstructed depth measurements are counted, never filled with zero.

The earlier depth/DOC-deviation association remains a useful field lead. It
concerns concentration relative to a flow-weighted incoming mixture, whereas the
current conservative experiment concerns peak timing and width. Conservative
routing does not by itself establish DOC production/removal or explain the two
observed seasonal sign changes. Five serial junctions also do not provide a
replicated comparison of the three whole-network forms.

## Research direction

The structure mechanism now has two explicit variables to take to observations:
**arrival separation relative to input duration**, and **shared response width
relative to input duration**. Keep path arrangement, junction placement and local
channel geometry as the explanatory centre. Maintain the original form labels as
the whole-network context.

The next useful empirical step is a joint conservative-tracer and laboratory-DOC
response at contrasting junction structures. The tracer can establish transit
and spreading; DOC relative to that response can identify an additional chemical
or exchange component. Actual conductivity breakthrough curves are preferable to
turning a surveyed depth ratio into an assumed storage constant. The existing
HydroShare inventory contains 189 public files, including oxygen-incubation
outputs, but the filename review did not locate a synchronized conservative/DOC
breakthrough pair. Incubation series should not be relabeled as river pulse curves.

For the river model, retain separate nonnegative directed arrival and shared
response operators. Their parameters should eventually be informed by transport
observations; the present scenarios establish which operations to distinguish,
not fitted coefficients for each real river.
