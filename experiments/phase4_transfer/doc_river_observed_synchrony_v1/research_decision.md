# Tributary excursions correspond to receiving excursions; whole-form mediation remains open

Date: 2026-10-08

## What this adds

The preceding real-path experiment showed how a river can separate synchronous
waves or align previously offset waves. This follow-up supplies **actual DOC
evidence for the input-coordination link**: receiving excursions are more likely
when multiple monitored tributaries are jointly high than when only one is high.

The main population remains 32 unique receiving networks, seven overlapping
catchment systems and 1,333 common months. Seventeen networks in six systems have
at least five coincident and five solo-source excursion dates; all 32 remain in
the source-coordination and receiving-amplitude panels. The shortest-route
version retains 32 networks, 1,330 months and 16 eligible excursion comparisons.
Source frontiers, original whole-network form classes and previous area-coverage
subsets remain unchanged. No new terrestrial source classification is introduced.

## 1. Actual joint highs and receiving response

Project native DOC on a shared calendar-season and linear-year design. A high
excursion is an anomaly above its own empirical 75th percentile, not an absolute
concentration threshold. Compare >=2 upstream sources high with exactly one high.

| Observational population | Receiving-excursion probability difference | 95% interval | Eligible receiving networks / systems |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full monthly panel | +19.6 percentage points | +10.7 to +40.6 | 17 / 6 |
| Represented area >=80% | +27.3 percentage points | +11.6 to +45.8 | 6 / 5 |
| Strict same-calendar-day activities | +17.0 percentage points | +1.0 to +28.6 | 3 / 3 |
| Saved shortest routes, monthly | +18.6 percentage points | +9.5 to +40.6 | 16 / 6 |

In the primary 17-network population, receiving-excursion probabilities average
44.4% on coincident-source dates and 24.8% on solo-source dates. Each receiving
network has one vote; these are not pooled date probabilities. There are 194
coincident and 261 solo dates across this eligible population. Thirteen of the
17 differences are positive and four are negative, all retained in the tables.
Intervals resample complete catchment-overlap systems 5,000 times.

Raw centered concentrations give +24.9 points [+13.7, +40.4] in 16 eligible
networks. The 90th-percentile monthly sensitivity is positive but only five
networks qualify, so it does not establish a general extreme-DOC result. The
1-day relaxed activity subset is less decisive (+14.2 points [-14.5, +35.7]);
the complete sampling-span grid remains visible rather than selecting a new cut.

The same-day subsets are small. Their observation-calendar selection and
activity-versus-monthly comparison are separately retained. These are concurrent
associations, not identified physical arrival times or causal source effects.

## 2. Same river, different input conditions

Use the previously metadata-selected Loch Vale case. Its two source paths,
shared corridor and lake context are fixed across years.

On 262 same-day observations in months with >=3 joint dates, after removing each
series' within-month mean:

- Coincident-source highs: receiving excursion on **26/42 dates (61.9%)**.
- Solo-source highs: receiving excursion on **12/48 dates (25.0%)**.
- Difference: **+36.9 points [+16.9, +59.2]**, resampling its 19 represented years.

The daily-mean replicate sensitivity gives 27/42 versus 11/48, a difference of
+41.4 points [+26.1, +57.8]. The complete 334-date calendar-adjusted case gives
+25.3 points [+9.6, +43.0] for selected activities. These populations and
adjustments remain separate.

Same calendar day is not synchronized sampling: all 334 selected date sets have
known timestamps, with a median station-to-station span of 2.68 hours and a
maximum of 5.87 hours. At least one upstream activity occurs after the receiving
activity on 130 dates. That clock audit was calculated from the saved selected
activity timestamps; it reinforces interpreting the result as co-fluctuation,
without assigning a travel time or directional causal coefficient.

Thirteen years support the within-month annual fluctuation comparison. Its
source-coordination/receiving-reference correlation is +0.541 [-0.022, +0.881].
The direction is suggestive but the interval includes zero. The dense case is
one lake-influenced, mainstem-dominated river, not replication of broad versus
elongated forms. The displayed waveform year is 2004, chosen by the largest
number of observed within-month dates and then the earliest year, not DOC peaks.

## 3. Keep the whole-network form question central

There is **no established difference in source coordination alone** between
the original broad and elongated classes. In the high-coverage subset the
broad-minus-elongated coordination difference is -0.032 [-0.185, +0.202].

The receiving fluctuation contrast remains a descriptive field lead. Normalize
receiving SD by sum(w_i * source SD_i), a perfectly coordinated source reference
that excludes source cross-covariance. In the fixed high-coverage population
(eight broad, four elongated networks), the broad/elongated geometric-mean
relative fluctuation is approximately **0.417 [0.212, 0.872]**. Regional context
and unmonitored inputs are still mixed with form; this is not an isolated shape
effect. It is a distinct denominator from the previous receiving/actual-mixture
contrast, whose historical results remain unchanged.

Measured source coordination versus this receiving reference is +0.155
[-0.185, +0.545] across all 32 networks. Mapped path CV and common-corridor
fraction also have inconclusive associations. The fixed five-year design gives
28 eligible periods, but only four rivers have repeated periods (11 periods,
three systems). Their receiver-centered correlation is +0.193 [-0.514, +1.000].
These records do not yet explain the whole-form field contrast through a fitted
mediation model.

Importantly, source covariance mechanically determines upstream-mixture
variance. This study uses an independent receiving signal and a reference
without pair covariance, rather than calling that source-mixture identity
evidence of downstream propagation.

## Research decision

Continue the structural explanation as:

**whole-network shape -> source-path and junction arrangement -> overlap of
arriving fluctuations -> shared-corridor smoothing -> receiving DOC.**

Actual tributary coincidence now supports the excursion-response link. A fixed
form ranking based only on tributary synchronization is not supported. Preserve
the receiving form contrast and investigate how paths act on those inputs.

The next useful evidence is additional co-sampled tributary/receiver records
with known timestamps in elongated and broad networks, retaining complete path
geometry and common-corridor waterbody context. First audit the already accepted
activity archive for additional complete same-day sets, including records that
cannot support a full monthly trend comparison. Then prioritize missing
observations among the existing same-region, similar-area form-pair candidates.
Use recorded hydrograph and sampling resolution to decide which time separation
can be resolved; do not tune a lag or dispersion strength to match the observed
class contrast. No new model training is warranted for this observational step.
