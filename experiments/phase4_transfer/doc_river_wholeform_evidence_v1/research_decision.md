# Whole-network form and DOC: current conclusion and the final field test

## Scientific answer

**River structure carries observed DOC information. A general DOC ranking of
elongated versus broad tributary-rich networks has not been established.**
These statements answer different questions. The strongest observed signal is
in continuous **branch organization**, rather than the visual footprint alone.
Controlled routing shows how geometry can modify an incoming pulse. Existing
event and confluence observations help specify that mechanism, but do not yet
identify a general whole-network form effect on field DOC events.

This synthesis reads existing results without refitting them. It also acquires
a new, public laboratory-DOC archive for an independent field comparison.

## 1. What has been established from the current records?

### Real forms exist and are measurable

The original geometry study classified 322 distinct receiving networks into
111 elongated tributary-rich, 33 mainstem-dominated sparse and 178 broad
tributary-rich forms. These are broad groups in continuous geometric space.
Nested upstream basins are not 322 independent ecosystems. Classification did
not use DOC. Retain the original definitions and measured geometries.

### Branch organization supplies extra observed DOC information

The existing 297-station, 62-HUC4 source-cohort diagnostic predicts **station
median DOC**, with the same environmental/area background and five HUC4 folds:

| Added information | Relative MAE reduction | Existing geographic interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| Whole-network footprint | 1.87% | -0.52 to 4.63% |
| Branch organization | 5.59% | 2.02 to 8.42% |
| Channel-path organization | 4.52% | -0.67 to 8.95% |
| All three structural blocks | 9.24% | 4.02 to 13.83% |

Branch organization also contributes 3.23% beyond the other structural blocks
(interval 0.86 to 5.14%). The existing analysis therefore supports additional
predictive information associated with river structure. It does not estimate a
causal concentration change. **The 9.24% is not an improvement of the released
monthly DOC reconstruction model.** This is an exploratory source-cohort
analysis, not a new external replication; basin nesting and unmeasured controls
remain relevant to interpretation.

### The direct broad-versus-elongated ranking remains unresolved

The 22 matched non-nested broad/elongated pairs span 12 HUC4 regions. Broad
minus elongated station median DOC is **+0.406 mg/L**, with interval
**-0.371 to +1.159 mg/L**. Variation and seasonal-amplitude contrasts also have
intervals crossing zero. The available comparison does not support assigning
one form a systematically higher DOC level or stronger buffering. It also does
not demonstrate that the forms are equivalent.

## 2. What does the mechanism work establish?

Actual path geometry changes the distribution of arrivals in controlled
identical-input routing. In the matched-network scenario, the broad forms have
more concentrated relative arrival paths and produce **higher normalized peaks
and narrower pulses** than elongated forms. This is conditional on the chosen
constant flow, identical pulses and normalized maximum travel delay. It is not
a measured storm response or a universal consequence of having many branches.

Later water/carbon-routing experiments show that unequal arrivals can lower a
peak, while spreading along a common downstream route can broaden a pulse.
Conservative routing retains the integrated carbon; a lower concentration peak
does not imply carbon removal. Broader responses need not have later peak times.

Observed upstream DOC is useful in the monthly monitored-source experiment,
but separating the two path-specific delays adds **-0.023%** relative MAE gain
over a common delay, interval **-0.058 to +0.067%**. This comparison has not
established extra overall information from the path-specific arrival mechanism.
The six elongated receivers all belong to one connected monitoring system;
the sparse class has one receiver in one system. Class averages cannot provide
independent whole-form confirmation.

The three high-frequency outlet archives contribute 128 positive DOC responses
and 112 complete width pairs. These remain observations within **three**
catchments; only Kervidy has a fully validated rooted vector map in that study.
The observed width differences do not establish a simple form ordering.

Five measured Tom's Creek confluences supply ten baseflow campaigns. Two
confluences reverse the sign of their DOC departure between seasons. Similar
local widening accompanies opposite DOC changes. Relative hydraulic residence
time is a descriptive lead, but these are **local channel dimensions in one
network**, not the user's elongated-versus-broad whole-network shapes.

The resulting explanation is specific:

> River structure organizes the routes and convergence of incoming DOC. The
> effect depends on differences among incoming waves, their water contributions,
> travel/residence distributions and transformations along the common route.
> A visual shape label alone does not determine the DOC response.

## 3. New independent-field data acquired in this version

The [NEON-authored public absorbance archive](https://zenodo.org/records/20039155)
contains laboratory DOC associated with 6,722 sample IDs at 34 aquatic sites,
from 21 October 2014 to 2 March 2026. Both published files were downloaded and
matched their published sizes/checksums. They are a public CC-BY derived archive;
the credential-requiring original NEON data download was not accessed.

Excluding the seven lake sites leaves:

- **27 stream/river sites**, across 17 NEON domains.
- **5,530 laboratory-DOC rows** before combining field replicates.
- **4,900 sampling occasions**, including relocated and depth-specific samples.
- **4,800 primary surface-location occasions**, with replicates averaged once
  per base sample ID. Base-sample dates and locations are consistent.
- **97 relocated occasions** retained in the inventory. Their exact alternative
  coordinates are absent, so they are excluded from primary-location summaries.
- A median of within-site median sample spacings of **13.95 days**.

No missing, negative or zero DOC appears in this derived subset. This does not
establish that the complete NEON DOC population has no missing/censored values.
The archive is selected for absorbance-related processing, so missingness and
selection relative to all chemistry samples need examination. Original quality,
sample-condition and detection-limit fields are not supplied. No new DOC form
effect is calculated from these records in this version.

The official [water-chemistry guide](https://data.neonscience.org/documents/10179/2838366/NEON_waterChem_userGuide_vF/de1c052b-cce3-ecdb-4459-48c60fbbb5d3)
documents numeric sample-ID suffixes as replicates and `RE` locations as samples
collected away from the designated station. Site-center coordinates were obtained
from the official public metadata endpoint. They are **not verified chemistry
outlet locations** and have not been used to assign river forms. The 27 sites
are candidates, not 27 confirmed independent networks.

The July 2024 guide describes `SS` for stream/river samples, while the distributed
BLWA, FLNT and TOMB records mostly use `C0`. The stored codes are preserved; the
coverage count uses conventional `SS`/`C0` positions and separately lists depth
and relocation codes. Actual chemistry locations must resolve this historical
coding difference before network extraction.

This archive provides a concrete route to **independent concentration and
variability validation**. Its approximately fortnightly grab samples cannot
verify subdaily event peak spreading or branch-arrival times.

## 4. The bounded next experiment and stopping point

Finish one independent field comparison before expanding the mechanism search:

1. Locate the actual chemistry outlets and their location histories. Obtain
   complete upstream channels and basin boundaries. Determine overlapping/nested
   networks and their observation units. Keep failures and small mapped networks
   in an acquisition ledger; exclude by geometry/record coverage rather than DOC
   outcomes. The existing coarse class labels accompany continuous measurements.
2. Compare DOC concentration level and temporal variation using comparable
   sampling calendars. Keep basin scale and environment/hydrology as comparison
   covariates; retain footprint, branching and path organization as separate
   explanatory blocks. With only a few dozen river sites, use a small declared
   model and leave whole independent networks out. Months and replicate bottles
   do not increase the spatial replication count.
3. Test whether the structural information found in ST357 persists in this new
   population, and whether any class contrast is repeatable. Report geometry
   ranges, overlap with ST357 and site sensitivity; do not select a different
   structural metric or subgroup because its DOC result is positive.

**Success is a repeatable structural contribution to at least one specified DOC
property after the measured comparison context is supplied.** It need not be a
universal ordering of all three forms or an effect on every response dimension.
This would establish an independent observational association and information
value, while a directional transport explanation would still need incoming-wave
measurements. Those are distinct scientific conclusions.

If complete geometry, appropriate chemistry locations/quality, comparison
support or independent network replication cannot be obtained, close that
comparison with the explicit reason. If the independent comparison finds no
supported additional structure information, retain it as the result and stop
expanding the broad morphology claim. Do not keep adding simulations to answer
a question that needs new field observations.

An event-scale causal-transport study would then be a separately scoped data
collection: concurrent branch/receiver DOC and discharge, measured arrivals and
repeated events in independent networks. It is not a prerequisite for reporting
the current predictive association, and cannot be completed by relabelling
fortnightly samples or outlet-only events.

are stored here; the raw public archive stays in the ignored data directory.
