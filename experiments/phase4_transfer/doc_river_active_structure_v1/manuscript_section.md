# River pathways and flow dependent DOC mixing

## Results

River form fixes the locations of branches and their paths to the receiver, but
the distribution of water entering those paths changes with hydrological
state. We tested this relationship on the original monitored frontiers without
changing their mapped paths or morphology assignments. Seventeen networks,
including seven elongated and ten broad tributary-rich networks, supported
contrasts between high and low receiver flow. The hydro analysis used 916
complete positive-flow network-months, while the corresponding DOC analysis
used 531 common observed network-months.

The effective contributor fraction, defined as the inverse squared flow-share
sum divided by the fixed monitored-source count, increased by 4.15 percentage
points at high flow after seasonal and linear-time adjustment (95% overlap-system
bootstrap interval 1.70–6.40 pp). This increase describes more balanced water
contributions among monitored branches. It does not count activation of
unmonitored or previously dry tributaries. The unadjusted change was 3.24 pp
(−0.16–6.40 pp), and no resolved difference was found between broad and
elongated networks (adjusted broad-minus-elongated contrast −2.04 pp,
−6.37–3.43 pp).

The participation result depended on monitoring coverage. Leaving each overlap
system out preserved a positive overall point estimate, but retaining only
networks with at least 80% represented catchment area reduced the mean to
−0.40 pp across four networks. Thus the positive full-panel response pertains
to the measured tributary combinations and is not a demonstrated whole-network
response. Flow-weighted path dispersion likewise showed no resolved aggregate
increase.

We separated DOC mixing into the contributions of mean water fractions, source
fluctuation amplitudes and source coordination. Using a common seasonal and
time projection for sources, their dynamic mixture and the receiver, the
fixed-weight mixing potential changed by −0.59 pp between high and low flow
(−6.83–6.62 pp). The analytic water-fraction contribution averaged +1.72 pp
(−0.50–4.25 pp), while the coordination contribution averaged −2.97 pp
(−9.71–6.28 pp). Individual networks showed both reinforcement and offsetting
of these contributions. Neither the aggregate DOC response nor its
broad-minus-elongated contrast was resolved by this panel.

## Discussion

These results connect the river-form hypothesis to an explicit mechanism:
mapped paths constrain where signals meet, while water fractions and the timing
and magnitude of branch DOC fluctuations determine the mixture presented to
the receiver. Equal incoming signals in controlled routing experiments isolate
the effect of path distributions under the chosen transport assumptions. In
field observations, the same static form can carry markedly different source
mixtures. Consequently, a more even supply of water need not produce a smoother
DOC series when branch fluctuations become more coordinated or change in
amplitude.

The current records identify changing participation among monitored tributaries
but do not establish a general DOC response specific to broad or elongated
forms. Monthly discharge fractions cannot resolve event arrival times, and
incomplete water budgets restrict extrapolation from a measured frontier to
the whole basin. A direct form test therefore requires near-complete branch
coverage and synchronized flow and DOC measurements in independent networks
of comparable catchment size and regional setting. Such measurements would
allow pathway dispersion, changing water contributions and source coordination
to be evaluated together across flood rises and recessions.
