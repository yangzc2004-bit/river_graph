# The missing link is co-sampled broad-network timing

Date: 2026-10-08

## Scientific purpose

Continue the whole-form explanation:

**river form -> path lengths and junction arrangement -> overlap of arriving
DOC fluctuations -> receiving peaks and smoothing.**

The preceding observational study established a positive association between
joint source excursions and receiving excursions. This step asks whether the
existing accepted archive supplies enough new, closely timed observations to
compare elongated and broad forms at that temporal scale. It does not introduce
a new land-cover or terrestrial-source classification.

## 1. The archive was extended beyond the previous monthly population

The audit reads 30,678 accepted sampling activities at 340 stations, restricted
to the established source-training cell union. Activity result replicates are
averaged, native DOC quality control is retained, and station-month means are
reconciled to the frozen dataset. All 297 mapped station-network instances
(295 receiving COMIDs) are assessed rather than only the earlier 32 included
monthly receivers.

Source selection uses dates, mapped disjoint catchments and represented area,
not DOC magnitudes. It no longer demands three sampled years and six calendar
months. Nevertheless, only three receivers have a nearest-gauge frontier with
12 exact common dates in three year-months:

| Receiving network | Original form | Joint dates | Dense within-month dates | Shared corridor / mean monitored path |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Trout River at Trout Lake, 05357245 | Mainstem dominated | 93 | 0 | 0.112 |
| Little Blue River, 06894000 | Elongated | 35 | 0 | 0.107 |
| Loch Vale outlet, 401733105392404 | Mainstem dominated | 334 | 262 | 0.867 |

These are existing monthly-analysis receivers, not three newly discovered
replicates. Loch Vale alone supports the dense within-month analysis. Its
262-date co-excursion result reproduces the preceding study exactly. The other
two receivers have one joint sampling date per sampled month, which cannot
resolve the within-month ordering of a high point.

## 2. The result is not an artifact of the nearest-gauge frontier

A sparsely sampled downstream gauge can conceal a better-sampled upstream
branch when only the nearest frontier is allowed. Therefore a separately
recorded metadata sensitivity checks **all non-nested candidate source pairs**.
It keeps source catchments disjoint and does not select pairs by DOC response.

| Original form | Candidate receiving networks | Non-nested gauge-pair combinations | Receivers meeting same-day signal counts | Receivers meeting dense within-month counts | Maximum common dates for one pair | Maximum dense dates for one pair |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Elongated | 17 | 164 | 2 | 0 | 57 | 3 |
| Mainstem dominated | 2 | 7 | 2 | 1 | 334 | 262 |
| Broad | 36 | 3,629 | 0 | 0 | 11 | 3 |

The 3,800 gauge-pair combinations reuse receivers and catchments. They are not
3,800 independent river networks. All date intersections and source-routing
ancestry have been independently reconstructed.

Broad networks **do have observations**: the richest allowed source-pair record
has 11 complete same-day dates. However, no broad pair supports a repeated
within-month timing comparison; the maximum dense count is only three dates.
The conclusion concerns this source-role archive, not all records ever collected
in these basins. Failing the signal-count rule is not evidence of a zero effect.

## 3. Identify where an informative form comparison could be built

The existing complete-network inventory supplies 105 same-HUC4 elongated/broad
pairs whose mapped catchment areas differ by at most a factor of two. No pair
currently has sufficient co-sampled records for both forms. Rank them by balanced
date availability, dense-date availability, total dates and area similarity,
without using DOC level or response direction.

Fourteen receiving pairs are nested along a larger river. For example, Kentucky
River lock stations describe different positions on the same main river. They
remain useful along-river cases but cannot be treated as independent parallel
form comparisons. A separate shortlist retains **91 non-nested receiving pairs**;
these are observation opportunities, not 91 independent experimental replicates.

The first non-nested availability-ranked pair is Little Blue River near Lake
City (elongated, 06894000) and Blue River at Blue Ridge Boulevard Extension
(broad, 06893150), HUC4 1030. Their mapped area ratio is 1.966. The elongated
receiver has a candidate source pair with 57 common dates; the broad receiver
has no complete two-source/receiver common-date set in the accepted archive.
This identifies a concrete observation gap, not a demonstrated shape effect.

The shortlist preserves every opportunity, availability count and nesting flag.
Regional proximity and area similarity alone do not isolate form; actual path
geometry, waterbody context and sampling resolution still need to be retained.

## Research decision

The next useful move is targeted observation recovery, especially for broad
networks, rather than repeatedly fitting a mediation model to the same monthly
panel. Start with the 91 non-nested form-pair opportunities and their explicit
missing source/receiver dates. Retrieve independent high-frequency or coordinated
sampling archives where available; evaluate availability before DOC outcomes.
If no archived record can resolve those timescales, prepare a co-sampling design
for disjoint upstream branches, their junctions and the receiver.

In parallel, retain the existing controlled real-path response experiments as
mechanism evidence. Their input timing is prescribed, whereas the current field
records measure actual concentrations. Neither is renamed as the other.

The project remains centered on **river geometry changing when fluctuations
overlap and how a shared corridor smooths them**. This step locates the field
evidence needed to test that explanation; it does not add a new confirmed
elongated-versus-broad effect, fitted transit time or transport coefficient.
