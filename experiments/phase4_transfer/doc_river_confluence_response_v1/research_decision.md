# Real confluences: water-wave overlap and the geometry of DOC mixing

## Research decision

The new observations make the structure question more concrete. Two incoming
water waves are not invariably synchronized, and local channel depth is a more
promising measured correlate of DOC mixture deviation than channel width in
this small field set. Continue along **incoming-wave arrangement plus local
post-junction geometry**, with the original elongated, broad-branching and sparse
whole-network forms retained as context.

This phase analyzes public observations. No DOC prediction model is retrained.
The two observation sets answer complementary questions; water timing is not
substituted for unmeasured carbon timing.

## 1. Incoming water waves at a real mapped junction

Eleven SITES half-hour discharge files were retrieved for all four previously
mapped Krycklan branch–branch–receiver configurations. All twenty original,
flow-selected spring windows are retained. Twelve meet 95% joint clock coverage
and retain each site's sampled maximum; eight have every half-hour slot observed.
All twelve eligible windows belong to **C2 + C4 → C7**. The other configurations
remain in the availability table; incomplete coverage or absence of a site's
maximum on the joint clock prevents extending the main comparison to them.

At C7, the two incoming normalized water profiles have an overlap of **0.709–0.883**
(median **0.785**). On the fully observed eight-window subset, median overlap is
**0.763**. Using a 10th-percentile baseline instead of the window minimum lowers
overlap by 0.001–0.061; it does not make the signals identical.

The recorded seasonal maximum clocks vary from almost simultaneous to about
15 days apart. These compare maxima in 29-calendar-day windows with multiple
water pulses. They are **not** travel times along the mapped 1.075-km independent
branch-path difference. We retain intervals for tied maxima, including separated
ties, rather than choosing one timestamp or optimizing a lag.

For the incoming excess-flow sum, its maximum divided by the sum of individual
branch maxima has median **0.951**. After balancing the two branch amplitudes,
the coincidence ratio ranges **0.758–0.999**, with median **0.917**. Thus a ratio
near one can reflect one dominant branch as well as temporal coincidence. Neither
ratio measures observed outlet peak attenuation. The two measured branches
contribute a median **0.672** of receiver flow across eligible window medians.
Their sum is a partial-input comparison.

**Meaning for river form:** branch paths and junction sequence act on incoming
signals that vary between years. A fixed network shape does not imply a fixed
peak response. The future test should ask how the same arrangement combines
synchronous versus staggered input waves, rather than assign each form a single
permanent DOC direction.

## 2. Measured local geometry and laboratory DOC at five confluences

The independent [Tom's Creek public archive](https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/c5e687fa040e4707ba922002bafd18fd/)
contains five serial confluences, two campaigns in 2021, thirty downstream
lateral-position means and 552 channel-survey rows. The latter represent 69
survey IDs, not 552 independent widths. The
[source methods](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022WR034224)
describe laboratory DOC and salt-tracer estimates of discharge and velocity;
flow was measured within three days after sampling. These are campaign snapshots,
not synchronized event waveforms. Fall geometry is compared only with fall DOC.

With one width per survey ID and equal weighting of survey depths, downstream
is wider than the mainstem at **3/5** confluences and deeper at **5/5**. Published
100-m normalized residence time is longer downstream at **4/5**. Normalized
residence time is a transit summary, not a separately identified storage volume.

Recomputed flow-normalized mixtures give the following deviations. Downstream
is the equal mean of three lateral positions, not a flux-weighted cross-section.

| Junction | Summer DOC deviation from mixture (%) | Fall DOC deviation (%) | Fall downstream/mainstem depth | Fall downstream/mainstem width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Con-1 | −4.85 | −13.73 | 1.425 | 1.592 |
| Con-2 | −4.66 | +19.49 | 1.652 | 1.630 |
| Con-3 | +12.01 | +5.36 | 1.545 | 1.189 |
| Con-4 | −10.76 | −3.12 | 1.530 | 0.998 |
| Con-5 | −2.42 | +10.46 | 1.536 | 0.912 |

Two junctions change DOC deviation sign between seasons. The reference mixture
lies within the three-position DOC range in only two of ten campaigns; the other
eight have the entire measured lateral range on one side of the reference.
Concentration deviations alone do not identify removal or production, especially
without a measured flux-weighted outlet concentration and uncertainties for all
incoming contributions.

### The useful geometry lead

For the **five fall junctions**, the rank correlation between relative channel
deepening and DOC deviation is **0.90**. Leaving out each junction in turn gives
**0.80–1.00**. Relative widening has correlation **0.10**, with leave-one ranges
**−0.80–0.40**. Width CV gives −0.40 and normalized residence-time ratio −0.60.
All four geometry relationships are retained in the descriptive table; none is
a causal coefficient or evidence from five independent catchments.

This supplies a specific hypothesis: **post-junction cross-section and contact
opportunity may matter beyond whether a network simply looks narrow or broad**.
The positive depth association means greater relative deepening corresponds to
a more positive DOC mixture deviation here; it does not mean deeper reaches
universally reduce DOC peaks. It is one campaign in a serial mainstem network,
so depth, flow, junction position and mixing cannot be disentangled by this
correlation alone.

## 3. Source details that affect interpretation

- One summer Con-1 campaign mixes supplied weight denominators. The new
  reference consistently normalizes by the sum of the two branch discharges;
  original row mixtures and sums remain alongside it.
- Thirty-one surveyed depths are missing at rock/tree obstructions and are
  counted, not filled with zero. Separate Con-1 channel/pool IDs remain separate;
  its CV differs from the published summary at this survey grain. The other
  four confluence width CVs replay the summary within rounding precision.
- One survey ID contains a 10–15-m distance conflict. Both bounds are preserved;
  no single location is invented. Its width is still constant.
- Conductivity deviations are −1.26% to +1.20%. However, downstream transects
  were selected for conductivity uniformity. This is a within-selected-transect
  diagnostic, not an independent proof of complete mixing or DOC transformation.
- Laboratory DOC is not interpolated to half-hour resolution. The public
  inventory found no complete, independently calibrated three-site DOC waveform
  ready for this natural-event comparison. Several useful archives instead offer
  branch-only sensors, sparse spatial samples or a single paired reach.

## 4. Next research step

Use the new **relative deepening** lead to investigate local junction structure
while keeping the whole-network classification fixed. In the existing controlled
routing framework, compare identical incoming carbon waves with:

1. different branch arrival separation but unchanged shared downstream geometry;
2. unchanged branch timing but altered post-junction cross-section and transit
   opportunity;
3. both changes together, retaining flow/amplitude conditions explicitly.

Measure peak overlap and duration separately from concentration change. Relate
these controlled sensitivities to the real network's branch paths, junction
sequence and shared reach. Do not convert an observed depth ratio directly into
a calibrated storage/removal constant.

In parallel, prioritize the publicly described confluence pulse-addition
experiments: a conservative tracer alongside directly sampled DOC can separate
translation/dispersion from carbon processing. Finding the original raw pulse
archive is the next targeted data task; the presentation lead itself is not new
quantitative evidence. This advances the **river-structure mechanism**, without
turning the project into a land-cover source-comparison study.
