# Water-state dependence of DOC mixing in monitored river arrangements

River geometry establishes opportunities for convergence, but branch water
contribution determines how strongly each path participates in the mixture. We
examined four actual confluence arrangements within the Krycklan catchment using
paired laboratory DOC and daily discharge, retaining both all eligible dates and
47 common sampling dates in 2014–2017. After removing the same annual-harmonic
and linear-time patterns, outlet-to-partial-mixture DOC SD ratios increased at
high flow at C7 and C16. The joint-calendar log-ratio changes were 0.626
(95% whole-year bootstrap interval 0.174–1.127) and 0.489 (0.192–1.282),
respectively. Similar ratio increases occurred on the complete calendars.

Separating the numerator and denominator changed the interpretation of these
responses. At C7, joint-calendar mixture SD declined from 5.141 to 3.149 mg C/L,
whereas outlet SD increased from 4.113 to 4.713 mg C/L. Thus, in the point
estimates, the increased ratio reflected a substantial change in the incoming
mixture as well as an outlet change. The component intervals were broad, so the
relative contribution of each change remains uncertain. An increased ratio
cannot alone be assigned to reduced channel smoothing or shorter residence time.

Branch coordination and water contribution acted in opposing directions in the
constructed mixing summaries. At C7, the mean contribution of branch A rose
from 0.109 at low flow to 0.334 at high flow. An all-order decomposition attributed
a positive 17.31-percentage-point contribution (11.67–23.34) to this change in
the fixed-weight mixture variance reduction, while the correlation contribution
was negative in the point estimate. The complete calendar likewise supported a
positive water-balance contribution of 15.31 percentage points (11.36–18.74).
At C16, complete-calendar mixing potential fell from 16.92% to 3.28%, and the
correlation contribution was −13.08 percentage points (−26.62 to −5.62), despite
a positive contribution from the changing water balance. These substitutions
describe an analytic summary and do not represent experimentally observed floods.

The four arrangements also leave a substantial unmonitored water contribution.
Median measured upstream-to-outlet discharge ratios on the joint calendar ranged
from 0.229 to 0.630. Most usable dates admitted nonnegative unmonitored-water
concentrations in a no-net-processing mixture equation. Those concentrations
were not observed, and this feasibility calculation does not identify actual
processing. It shows why measured branch mixtures and outlet DOC alone cannot
close the channel carbon budget.

Together, the observations motivate a representation combining separate and
shared river paths, branch water participation, and DOC signal coordination.
The contrasting responses within a fixed arrangement show why a static network
shape label is insufficient to describe its water-state-dependent DOC behavior.
These nested configurations provide process-oriented case evidence; independently
replicated elongated and broad/branched networks with complete event-scale water
and carbon observations are the next test of the whole-network morphology
hypothesis.
