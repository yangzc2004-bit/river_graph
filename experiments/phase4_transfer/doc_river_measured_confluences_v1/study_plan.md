# Measured confluence geometry and DOC mixing

## Question and scope

Test whether observed DOC downstream of real confluences is explained by
measured incoming water and DOC, and whether the remaining departure varies
with measured channel geometry and water residence time. This follows the
fixed-input geometry and carbon-budget scenarios with independently collected
field measurements. River structure remains the explanatory target.

Use every one of the five Tom's Creek confluences in both published summer and
fall 2021 campaigns. Select the public Plont archive by its upstream, tributary,
downstream, tracer-hydrology and geometry coverage before calculating its DOC
departures. This is a reanalysis of published field data, not a new field
experiment, an independent reproduction of its authors' conclusion, or an
external DOC prediction-model validation.

The campaigns describe baseflow spatial snapshots. They cannot estimate event
peak lag, width, annual export, or rank elongated versus broad whole networks.
Tracer flow was measured within three days of chemistry, not instantaneously
at every chemistry sample. The previous Turbolo hourly case remains historical
context; its documented optical retrieval and nearly scaled flow series do not
provide independent branch–branch–outlet event inputs.

## Comparisons fixed before calculation

For each confluence and campaign, preserve upstream, tributary, downstream
transect and any additional mixing-zone samples separately. Read author
metadata and analysis code to identify their grouping; do not count laboratory
replicates or transect positions as independent river networks.

1. Compute the two incoming DOC fluxes, their sum and the observed downstream
   flux using explicit mg C/L and L/s units. Report the water closure ratio.
2. Compare downstream concentration with both the input-flow-normalized mixture
   and incoming carbon divided by measured downstream flow. Report DOC
   concentration departure and bounded spatial flux discrepancy separately.
3. Apply the same calculation to specific conductivity as a reference for
   mixing and measurement alignment. Also calculate a conductivity-derived
   water fraction where it lies within the source endpoints; flag extrapolation
   and weak endpoint separation. Do not silently clip a tracer fraction.
4. Quantify within-transect variability, retaining actual sample positions and
   observations. Compare mixing-zone heterogeneity with fully mixed downstream
   and incoming reaches where available.
5. Relate DOC departure to measured downstream/upstream residence-time ratio,
   tributary water contribution, and fall channel width/depth variability.
   Source DOC contrast is an explicit mixing covariate, not a replacement for
   geometry. Report associations descriptively and leave-one-confluence
   sensitivity; the five confluences share a network.

Report all ten confluence-campaigns, including directions that disagree with
the hypothesis. Sampling uncertainty can resample positions within each reach
only as a measurement-sensitivity calculation. It does not create independent
events or warrant a population-wide river-form effect.

## Deliverables and interpretation

Preserve public file inventory, selected raw objects, units and acquisition
records; produce a one-command analysis, a compact English research decision,
Chinese/English scientific figures and independent calculation checks. Keep
source scripts read-only and do not execute their R code.

Interpret departure as observed non-conservative mixing or unaccounted input
under the stated sampling conditions. DOC disappearance cannot be separated
from other inputs, hydrology uncertainty and reaction using a concentration
departure alone. Geometry comparisons may establish a useful field lead; the
future decisive event experiment still needs synchronized branch and outlet
DOC/Q with mapped paths and lateral-input accounting.
