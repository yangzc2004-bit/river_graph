# Field data for the river form and DOC peak comparison

Date: 2026-10-09

## Question and scope

Can newly identified public observations support the next empirical comparison
of DOC peak concentration, response duration and timing in elongated and broad
river networks? The existing 32 ST357 frontiers and Krycklan results are known.
This is a new source and observation-resolution study, not a new model test or
confirmation of a morphology effect.

Audit public source families identified in the current literature search:
NEON surface-water chemistry and water-quality sensors; the 2026 Arctic Data
Center high-frequency and storm-event packages for Upper Kuparuk and Oksrukuyik;
the Kervidy-Naizin high-frequency archive; and the published daily optical DOC
archive for Krycklan and Yli-Nuortti. Preserve source methods and distinguish
laboratory DOC from calibrated optical estimates. Do not repeat the bounded
search of old USGS station archives.

## Acquisition and field eligibility

Use primary repository metadata and public object URLs. Record downloaded
versions, file identities, units, clocks, missing codes and author-provided
processing. Do not request or search for private credentials. A source requiring
an account remains an access limitation while other public sources are audited.

A direct form comparison needs actual mapped whole-network geometry, multiple
independent networks across forms, comparable scale and setting, and repeated
complete events with flow and DOC. Attribution to upstream arrival or mixing
also needs concurrent monitored branches and the receiving station with water
coverage. Outlet-only data can support observed response shapes but cannot
separate branch mixing from changing inputs.

The original morphology definitions stay unchanged. A published lake versus
river catchment label is not substituted for elongated versus broad form.
Do not assign form classes from observed DOC responses. Spatial synoptic samples,
daily aggregates and sub-hourly records retain separate eligibility.

## High frequency record audit

Retrieve the two 2026 Arctic Data Center packages using their published DOIs.
Read all tabular entities and attribute definitions. Audit station/year records,
timestamp uniqueness, cadence, units, finite positive flow, available DOC,
processing flags and gaps. Retain invalid or incomplete records in a separate
ledger rather than interpolating them.

Use the independently published event start/end intervals if supplied by the
storm package. First assess coverage at those unchanged intervals. An event is
coverage-qualified if it has at least 24 elapsed hours, at least 90% of expected
records on the source grid, both DOC and positive flow at the required records,
and no internal gap longer than one hour. Report all attempted intervals and
failure reasons. Repeated events remain nested in their original catchments.

Before interpreting event peak duration, inspect the actual series and source
baseline definition. Coverage eligibility alone does not establish a full DOC
rise and recovery, an upstream-to-downstream lag or an independent form effect.

## Deliverables

Save a source suitability table, retrieved-entity inventory, station/year
coverage and event audit where source fields permit it. Produce an English
research decision explaining which comparison is now possible, the remaining
geometric or simultaneous-observation gap, and the next concrete analysis.
Keep old results intact and use only this versioned directory for new products.
