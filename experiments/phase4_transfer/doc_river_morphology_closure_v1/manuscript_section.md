# River-network geometry organizes DOC fluctuations through arrival overlap and downstream spreading

## Results

River-network morphology offers a spatial explanation for how DOC signals
combine, beyond the environmental conditions that generate them. We retained
the previously mapped elongated, mainstem-dominated and broad tributary-rich
forms and represented their monitored inputs using actual channel paths to a
receiving station. Controlled experiments on 32 receiving networks separated
arrival-path differences from spreading along the shared downstream corridor.
All sources received the same unit-amplitude pulse, with fixed source shares
and an explicitly imposed uniform-speed time scale.

For a pulse with standard deviation equal to 0.1 of mean nominal travel time,
unequal arrival paths reduced the mean receiving peak by 21.6% compared with
equalized arrivals (95% overlapping-system bootstrap interval, 15.1–26.9%
reduction), while increasing central response duration. Introducing a causal,
unit-gain spreading kernel on the actual common corridor reduced the peak a
further 32.4% relative to the unequal-path response without spreading
(27.6–35.1%). This operation preserved each path's mean arrival time and total
anomaly input. A reduced peak therefore represented a wider temporal response.
For inputs varying slowly relative to path time, corresponding peak changes
were less than 1%. Structural buffering was strongest when the input fluctuated
on a time scale comparable to, or shorter than, the differences among arrivals.

Source timing supplied a complementary effect. Advancing each source pulse to
compensate for its longer route aligned arrivals and increased the receiving
peak relative to synchronous inputs on the same unequal paths. The network's
response thus depended jointly on path arrangement and the timing of its
inputs. Moving the junction while leaving complete path lengths and
constant-speed translation unchanged did not alter peak or duration. A junction
effect emerged only when the changed common corridor carried an additional
spreading operation. These experiments identify the transport quantities behind
the visual form rather than assuming that a greater number of branches always
implies stronger buffering.

Actual DOC observations supported the overlap-to-excursion link. Across 17
receivers with sufficient coincident and solo source excursions, the probability
of a receiving excursion averaged 44.4% when at least two monitored upstream
sources were high and 24.8% when only one was high. The receiver-equal difference
was 19.6 percentage points (95% interval, 10.7–40.6). Thirteen receivers showed a
positive difference; four showed the opposite direction. Removing each eligible
catchment-overlap system in turn retained a positive mean difference of
16.7–28.3 points and intervals above zero. This association persisted beyond
one particular river system. It concerns sampled, season/year-adjusted
75th-percentile excursions; the records do not reconstruct unsampled event
maxima or identify physical transit times.

The identical-input broad-versus-elongated peak contrast remained unresolved.
For the 0.1-duration pulse, the broad-minus-elongated difference was −0.061
(95% interval, −0.202 to 0.098); introducing the declared common spreading gave
−0.011 (−0.100 to 0.073). Targeted public-record recovery for 15 same-region,
similar-area non-nested form opportunities retrieved records from 43 stations
and used one recorded archive fallback, yielding 5,640 accepted activities.
None of the 15 opportunities supplied adequate two-tributary/receiver
co-sampling in both forms. The recovery defines the missing field comparison
without selecting new class boundaries or weakening temporal-resolution rules.
An additional public-station inventory supplied 49 archives beyond ST357 and
1,047 accepted activities in two broad catchments; neither channel-mapping
sensitivity resolved the co-sampling gap. Across both recoveries, 6,687 accepted
activities were inspected. Six regional catalogue requests remained inaccessible,
so this bounded recovery is not an exhaustive inventory of all monitoring
providers. The current USGS parameter-00681 time-series catalogue supplied no
continuous DOC series at the target receivers.

## Discussion

These results support a structural interpretation of DOC variability in which
river-network geometry organizes the overlap of arriving signals and their
subsequent spreading. The effect is inherently scale dependent. Unequal paths
can distribute a short common input over time; source timing can also offset
those differences and concentrate arrivals. A shared downstream corridor can
broaden the combined signal when it provides a relevant mixing or storage
process. Whole-network morphology is a useful organizing description, while
path-length dispersion, confluence arrangement and the process properties of
the shared corridor supply the operative information.

This framework connects three complementary kinds of evidence. Real mapped
networks define the structural arrangement. Controlled, input-preserving
experiments isolate its temporal consequences. Sampled upstream and receiving
DOC establish that coordinated source excursions accompany increased receiving
excursion frequency across monitored systems. Existing paired conservative and
labelled-carbon releases further show that transport-induced spreading and
carbon-specific response changes can occur separately. None of these layers
requires changes in terrestrial source composition to define the structural
operation being tested.

The next empirical advance is a coordinated comparison of whole forms on the
relevant temporal scale. Comparable elongated and broad catchments should be
sampled at non-nested tributaries, junctions and downstream receiving reaches,
with synchronized clocks, DOC, discharge and a conservative reference. Repeated
events would distinguish separation among source arrivals from spreading after
confluence and chemical departures from conservative transport. Such a design
would directly connect whole-network form to measured peak concentration and
response duration, building on the mechanism established here.
