# Observed DOC buffering depends on water state and the fluctuation component

To connect mapped river arrangement with measured DOC variability, we extended
the spring-window comparison to the complete laboratory archive at four monitored
Krycklan confluences. The 561 eligible three-site comparisons included 47 calendar
dates shared by all four arrangements during 2014–2017. Each comparison retained
the measured DOC and daily-flow-weighted mixture of its two monitored branches.
All concentrations were projected onto the same annual harmonic and linear-time
design, allowing variation around the calendar pattern to be distinguished from
total concentration variation.

On shared dates, adjusted outlet-to-mixture SD ratios were 0.860, 0.959, 1.471 and
0.881 at C12, C16, C7 and C9, respectively. Whole-year bootstrap intervals supported
lower variation at C12 (0.843–0.886) and greater variation around the calendar
pattern at C7 (1.154–2.217). At C7, however, the unadjusted ratio was 0.783: the
seasonal/time design accounted for 83.2% of mixture variance and 40.5% of outlet
variance. An apparently buffered seasonal pattern therefore coexisted with larger
remaining outlet fluctuations relative to the measured branch mixture. This
direction persisted after separately omitting each observed year.

The response also depended on water state within the same mapped arrangement.
Adjusted ratios increased from 0.632 at low flow to 1.030 at high flow at C16,
and from 0.800 to 1.497 at C7. The paired high-minus-low differences were 0.399
(0.155–0.759) and 0.697 (0.242–1.100); both directions recurred in the full-calendar
sensitivity. These observations motivate conditioning a structural response on
flow and incoming signal coordination, rather than assigning one buffering
strength to each outline class.

The four arrangements are nested within one catchment, and their monitored
branches provide only part of receiving discharge. Consequently, the comparisons
describe temporal repeatability and partial-input signal relations; they do not
identify carbon loss, hydraulic transit time or a population-level geometry
effect. Event observations of both tributaries and the receiving reach would
test how path differences, arrival overlap and shared-corridor exposure produce
the conditional responses found here.
